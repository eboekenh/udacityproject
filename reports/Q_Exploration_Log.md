## Reflection (include in written summary)

- **Where did Q agree with the dashboard?** On 4 of 5 questions, Q's natural-language answers matched the corresponding dashboard visuals exactly or near-exactly — including specific figures like YieldMax Software's 334 tickets/$40,722 (Q4), the full campaign spend-vs-revenue ranking (Q5), the Enterprise/Small/Medium/Large deal-size ordering (Q2), and the Direct Mail conversion/response-rate leadership (Q1). This is strong evidence that the Topic's relationships (account_id joins across CRM, Marketing, and Support datasets) were configured correctly.
- **Where did Q disagree or struggle? Why?** On Q3 (resolution time by priority), Q's directional finding was correct (critical resolves faster than low) but the ticket-count figures it cited (819 critical / 26,706 low) were an order of magnitude off from the data dictionary's true per-ticket counts (50 critical / 1,500 low). This is most likely a fan-out effect: because the Topic joins Support Tickets to CRM Deals on account_id, and several accounts have many deals, ticket rows can be multiplied when Q aggregates across the joined model. It's a useful caution that natural-language answers involving counts (as opposed to rates or rankings) should always be spot-checked against the underlying dataset when cross-dataset relationships are involved.
- **When would you use Q vs. the dashboard to answer a business question?** Q is fastest for one-off, natural-language questions, especially ranking/comparison questions ("which channel is best?", "who are our top at-risk accounts?") where the underlying visual would otherwise need to be built from scratch — and it also handles the Reference Documents question (Q1 of the Verification Log) that no dashboard visual could answer at all. The dashboard is more reliable for anything involving raw counts across joined tables, since it exposes exactly which fields and grains are being aggregated, avoiding the fan-out ambiguity observed in Q3.
